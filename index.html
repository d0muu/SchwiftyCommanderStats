Meta Analysis
🌿 Speed Wins This Meta
The May–Jul sessions show the meta evolving toward faster, more explosive finishes. T4 Hulk Combo (Dom/Henzie), Peer into the Abyss (Terry/Doctor Doom), Squirrel Girl pop-off (James) — games are ending earlier and saltier. Board wipes, mana lock, and global damage remain common. The meta still rewards proactive play but the definition of "proactive" now includes combo.
🔮 The Rotation Problem
Dom has now played Rev, Slicer, Helga, Mogis, Nekusar, Nath, Maelstrom Wanderer, and Henzie across the last few sessions — Arcades and Monk Gyatso remain undefeated and seemingly never played, through 99 games. Frenchy meanwhile is running multiple commanders per session (Y'shtola, Zo-Zu, Auntie Ool, Aminatou, Alela, Gahiji, Queen Marchesa) and finding wins with several of them. The rotation paradox continues: Frenchy rotates and wins; Dom rotates and sometimes wins now too (Henzie combo, Vihaan), but also scoops to go to the toilet.
🩸 The Kill Economy
299 total kills across 101 games — 2.96 per game, stable all season. Split: 176 combat (59%), 100 alternate (33%), 28 commander damage (9%). The Wilson vs Terry kill race: 67–55, gap now 12.
🎯 The Coalition Problem
The Jun 6 G3 Nath game is the clearest coalition example of the season: Dom salted the entire table into coordinating against him, and they did. "The fun police" got a mana lock going, had 5 salty players at the table, and lost anyway. Frenchy led the political coalition to kill Dom. This is the meta in microcosm — the most aggressive political play generates the most coordinated response.
📅 The November Outlier
November 15 (15 games, 41 kills) remains the record session. The Jun 6 session produced 6 games in a single night — the second densest session of the season. Between them these two sessions account for 21 of 99 total games. The group plays harder when everyone shows up.

⚖ The Political Meta
The Schwifty table has a distinct political culture: alliances form fast, collapse faster, and "world peace" is usually Frenchy's idea. The dominant pattern is two-player blocs vs a shared threat, most commonly the table coordinating against Wilson or Terry. But Wilson keeps winning anyway ("We all almost got Wilson, down to 3hp"), suggesting the coalitions either break down at the wrong moment or lack the firepower to close.
🗡 The Only Confirmed Betrayal
The dataset contains exactly one clear betrayal: Dom vs Mitch, September 2025. Dom fed Mitch cards to keep him in the game as a buffer. Mitch took Dom out "first chance cause I had some counters." This founding act of treachery explains a significant portion of the Dom-Mitch friction that persists through the entire season — Dom has killed Mitch 6 times since, Mitch has killed Dom 6 times back. It's an arms race with receipts.
🤝 World Peace — The Great Cope
"World peace" recurs throughout the game log — including Dom's Jun 25 deal with Mitch, struck with Laboratory Maniac already in hand. It is never quite peaceful. The Jan 22 world peace occurred because "Frenchy could have won that next turn" — meaning it was less a mutual agreement and more a desperate stop-Frenchy coalition with diplomatic branding. The Sep 13 "Fake World Peace — Frenchy" entry speaks for itself. The Jan 11 world peace ("Would've been too grindy") is the only one that feels genuine. Frenchy originates or is directly involved in most of the table's world-peace events — though the latest, Dom–Mitch on Jun 25, happened while he was away.
👑 Kingmaker Central — Mitch and Wilson
Mitch is the group's most active kingmaker. His February 2026 note is definitive: "Roast lobster all game from TK, then kingmakes him cockblocking James yet again." He also Seppuku'd specifically to deny Terry a kill in November. Wilson begged Frenchy to ping him for 1 damage in January specifically to deny Mitch the kill. The group plays politics even in their death throes — and Mitch consistently ends up choosing the winner rather than becoming one.
🌶 The Wilson-Frenchy Axis
The most politically significant relationship in the dataset is Wilson and Frenchy in cahoots. Dom's January 2026 note reads: "Frenchy and Wilson in cahoots this game" with Wilson winning via Chiss-Goria. Mitch's note from the same game: "Once again Wilson and Frenchy ruin it for everyone — Wilson: '...that's true.'" This confirms a recurring informal alliance that the rest of the table is aware of but hasn't been able to consistently break up. They kill each other plenty (Frenchy 8× Wilson kills, Wilson 7× Frenchy kills) but when they align, they're the most dangerous coalition at the table.
